REFERENCE
F
Flood and inundation screening

Open water often changes radar backscatter, but flooded vegetation, wind, incidence angle and soil conditions complicate interpretation.

A
Agriculture and soil moisture context

Repeat passes can capture field roughness, crop structure and wetness changes. A single intensity image is not a direct soil-moisture measurement.

C
Coastal / river morphology

Useful for wetland, shoreline and river-channel context. Always consider acquisition geometry, tidal stage and seasonal variation.

Not a flood alertThis app displays data and screening tools. It does not issue official flood warnings or infer inundation automatically from raw scene metadata.
Aznur RadarLens is a research prototype. Sentinel-1 RTC is a free public radar archive through Microsoft Planetary Computer; scene metadata and raster tiles require internet access and may be throttled. Synthetic demo records are marked explicitly.

Pre/post comparison checklist
For rigorous change detection
01
Match orbit and polarization

Use the same relative orbit where possible, pass direction, polarization and compatible processing product.

02
Apply terrain and radiometric corrections

Use a documented RTC product, and account for layover, shadow, steep terrain and incidence geometry.

03
Reduce speckle appropriately

Use a justified filter or multi-temporal method without erasing small features or sharp boundaries.

04
Validate with other evidence

Check rainfall, gauge levels, optical imagery when available, field reports and known flood extents.

Not an operational flood map

Backscatter change alone does not prove inundation. Flooded vegetation, wind-roughened water, wet soil, tides, urban geometry and image acquisition time can cause confusing signals.
